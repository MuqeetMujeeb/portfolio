# Build the professional UI as an AI-lab notebook

The best professional design for an early-career AI Engineer's portfolio is a **warm, light, editorial "lab notebook"**. It uses a cream canvas, a serif display face paired with a clean sans, one non-purple accent, and dark panels only for code, metrics and the chatbot. Each project is a case study with architecture diagrams and latency, eval and cost numbers. This look follows the visual language AI labs have adopted (Anthropic/Claude, Replicate, ElevenLabs) and the minimal editorial style of respected AI-adjacent builders (Lee Robinson, Paco Coursey, Emil Kowalski, swyx). It avoids the dark-navy, purple-gradient, bento-grid and Brittany Chiang-clone looks that now read as templates or "AI slop." The reasoning: reviewers judge visual appeal in about **50 ms**, and "design look" is the most-cited credibility factor. Hiring managers, though, open portfolios mostly to break ties, and they reward proof of shipping and evals over effects. So the professional UI should be restrained and quick to skim, and dense with evidence. The site already has a playful medieval UI behind a switch, so the professional default does not need to be memorable through novelty. It can put all its effort into clarity and proof. The chatbot should be the one signature interaction. It works best as an "Ask my portfolio ⌘K" command palette that answers with citations, not a floating support bubble. Ranked: (1) warm editorial lab notebook, (2) research/case-study notebook, used as the template for project pages, (3) dark technical without the clichés, offered as the dark theme rather than the default, (4) light product-landing with subtle motion. Bento, terminal and 3D styles should be used for one section at most, or left out.

## Reviewers skim for 10–60 seconds and look for proof, not polish

Recruiter attention is short and patterned. In the Ladders eye-tracking study of 30 recruiters, an initial resume screen averaged **7.4 seconds**. The best performers had simple layouts, clear headings, bold titles and white space. Crowded designs, multiple columns and long paragraphs did worst ([HR Dive](https://www.hrdive.com/news/eye-tracking-study-shows-recruiters-look-at-resumes-for-7-seconds/541582/)). Critics note the method is thinly reported, so treat the exact figure as a rough guide ([StandOut CV](https://standout-cv.com/stats/how-long-recruiters-spend-looking-at-cv)). Web research points the same way: people judge a page's visual appeal within **50 ms**, and those snap judgments correlate strongly with ratings made at 500 ms ([Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Attention-web-designers:-You-have-50-milliseconds-a-Lindgaard-Fernandes/f9715b117c57d4e7064afe1c1cb95d5bf4cc1831)). In the Stanford credibility study, **"design look" appeared in 46.1% of comments**, more than any other factor, followed by information structure ([Stanford PDF](http://credibility.stanford.edu/pdf/How_Do_People_Evaluate_a_Web_Site's_Credibility_v37.pdf)). No study has tracked recruiters' eyes on engineer portfolio sites specifically, so these findings are transferred from resumes and general websites.

The portfolio's role is narrower than most developers assume. One hiring manager said they open portfolios mainly for candidates who are a "borderline no" or a possible perfect fit ([Blind](https://www.teamblind.com/post/do-you-look-at-candidates-portfolio-zlbuydfd)). A survey of 60+ hiring managers found that most would look at a site if one was provided, but not having one made little difference. The same managers warned that "most developers aren't born designers," and that broken links and stale content hurt ([DEV / Profy](https://dev.to/profydev/this-survey-among-60-hiring-managers-reveals-don-t-waste-your-time-on-a-react-portfolio-website-17ge)). The site is a tie-breaker, so its job is to **remove doubt quickly**. Within the first screen it must say who he is, the target role in searchable words ("AI Engineer — LLMs, RAG, voice agents"), and show one or two numbers that prove he ships.

AI hiring managers ask for something specific: deployed work plus measurement. Practitioner guides from 2026 agree that a live endpoint beats a notebook. They also say the eval story (golden datasets, LLM-as-judge, regression tests, monitoring) is what "separates a senior-signal portfolio from a junior one. Almost nobody does this" ([hireagentic.dev](https://hireagentic.dev/blog/hire-ai-engineer-guide); [ai-tldr.dev](https://ai-tldr.dev/learn/building-ai-apps/ai-career-path/build-ai-portfolio/)). Eugene Yan, now at Anthropic, lists evals, monitoring and data literacy among his core hiring signals ([Eugene Yan](https://eugeneyan.com/writing/how-to-interview/)). NN/g's survey of 200+ hiring managers found that juniors are judged on process and seniors on scope ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/ux-portfolios-hiring/)). For this candidate, that means showing **trade-offs and failure analysis**, not just results. The design implication is that the layout must make room for these artifacts: latency budgets for the voice agent (STT → LLM → TTS, time-to-first-audio), recall@k and faithfulness for RAG, p95 latency for the real-time backend, and architecture diagrams. Feature cards with icons have no room for any of that.

## Respected AI builders run plain sites; design engineers set the visual bar

Among the people AI engineers admire most, a heavily designed portfolio is rare. Karpathy's site is one hand-written page with a generic `sans-serif` font and a timeline ([karpathy.ai](https://karpathy.ai)). Lilian Weng uses a stock Hugo theme ([lilianweng.github.io](https://lilianweng.github.io)). Hamel Husain and Jason Liu run docs-site setups on Quarto and MkDocs ([hamel.dev](https://hamel.dev); [jxnl.co](https://jxnl.co)). Their credibility comes from books, open-source work and writing. An early-career engineer does not have that reputation yet. The usable lesson is the **content mechanics**, not the plainness. Eugene Yan places live prototypes (AlignEval, AI Reading Club) alongside essays and uses "More than 50k reads" as social proof ([eugeneyan.com](https://eugeneyan.com)). Jason Liu leads with one named open-source tool and an outside endorsement ([jxnl.co](https://jxnl.co)). Husain anchors his whole site on one niche ([hamel.dev](https://hamel.dev)).

The visual standard comes from design engineers, and it has converged on **minimal editorial**. That means a narrow column, a serif and sans pairing, neutral grays with at most one accent, light and dark theme metas, and a bio → projects → writing → contact order. Lee Robinson, who has moved from Vercel to Cursor to ML at SpaceX, pairs Geist with an Iowan Old Style reading serif on warm paper tones `#f8f6f0` ([leerob.com](https://leerob.com)). Paco Coursey pairs Newsreader with Inter on a Radix gray scale and puts a ⌘K project up front ([paco.me](https://paco.me)). Emil Kowalski uses near-black `#0B0B09` on off-white `#F5F4F4` with custom serif and sans faces ([emilkowal.ski](https://emilkowal.ski)). swyx uses Cardo and Newsreader on cream `#f5f0e7`, with a "Search the notebook…" ⌘K box ([swyx.io](https://swyx.io)). The counter-example is Brittany Chiang's dark slate-900 site with a teal accent and sticky two-column layout. It works well but is "the most-imitated developer portfolio on the internet," so copies read as templates ([AdminLTE](https://adminlte.io/blog/react-portfolio-templates/); [brittanychiang.com](https://brittanychiang.com)). Bruno Simon's drivable Three.js world is memorable but hard to skim ([bruno-simon.com](https://bruno-simon.com)). That is the role the medieval UI already fills.

The AI companies themselves have split into two families. "Dark technical" (Linear, Vercel-dark) uses a near-black canvas `#010102`, a surface ladder `#0f1011`→`#191a1b`, hairlines `#23252a` and one accent. Its rules are explicitly "no gradients, no second chromatic accent" ([awesome-design-md / Linear](https://github.com/VoltAgent/awesome-design-md/blob/main/design-md/linear.app/DESIGN.md)). "Warm editorial" is the lab look. Claude uses a cream canvas `#faf9f5`, ink `#141413`, a coral accent and a 64px serif display. Dark surfaces are kept for code windows rather than inverting the whole page ([awesome-design-md / Claude](https://github.com/VoltAgent/awesome-design-md/blob/main/design-md/claude/DESIGN.md)). The identity agency describes this as "a humanist serif for reading… with a clean sans for headlines… the inverse of the usual tech convention" ([Geist agency](https://geist.co/work/anthropic)). Replicate (cream `#f9f7f3`, hot orange `#ea2804`, dark code bands) and ElevenLabs (light serif display, Inter body) belong to the same family ([Replicate](https://github.com/VoltAgent/awesome-design-md/blob/main/design-md/replicate/DESIGN.md); [ElevenLabs](https://github.com/VoltAgent/awesome-design-md/blob/main/design-md/elevenlabs/DESIGN.md)). These tokens come from a community extraction, not official brand documents, so treat the hex values as approximate. OpenAI, Modal, LangChain and LiveKit were not checked.

What to avoid is now well documented. Design writers list the tells of "AI slop": Inter used by default, the blue-to-purple gradient ("the single loudest AI tell in 2026," traced to Tailwind's indigo-500), a centered hero with a pill badge, and three rounded cards with line icons ([925 Studios](https://www.925studios.co/blog/ai-slop-design-tells); [SmoothUI](https://smoothui.dev/blog/ai-design-slop)). Creative Boom's 2026 list of trends designers are tired of includes glassmorphism, gradients everywhere, bento grids, "minimalism without soul," and "motion for motion's sake" ([Creative Boom](https://www.creativeboom.com/insight/10-trends-creatives-are-so-over-in-2026/)). Bento is called "arguably the defining UI style of 2025 and 2026," which is exactly why it now feels tired ([Desinance](https://desinance.com/design/bento-grid-web-design/)). It is also the default layout of off-the-shelf RAG-chatbot portfolio templates ([medevs/smart-portfolio](https://github.com/medevs/smart-portfolio)).

## Four directions, ranked, with concrete specs

The table below compares the candidate directions. The rules shared by every serious system carry across all of them: a 4px spacing base, roughly 96px between sections, negative letter-spacing on display type, one chromatic accent reserved for CTAs, links and focus rings, hairline borders instead of heavy shadows, and monospace only for technical labels ([Vercel](https://github.com/VoltAgent/awesome-design-md/blob/main/design-md/vercel/DESIGN.md); [Linear](https://github.com/VoltAgent/awesome-design-md/blob/main/design-md/linear.app/DESIGN.md)).

| Rank | Direction | Exemplars | Type | Color | Layout | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Warm editorial "lab notebook" | Claude/Anthropic, Replicate, swyx.io, leerob.com | Instrument Serif or Newsreader display + Geist/IBM Plex Sans UI + JetBrains Mono | Canvas `#faf9f5`, ink `#141413`, body `#3d3d3a`, one accent (rust `#c2410c`, amber or signal green, not coral or purple) | Single column, ~680–720px text measure; dark "chrome" panels for code, metrics, chat | Looks like a Claude clone if the accent is coral; "lazy minimalism" if thin on content |
| 2 | Research/case-study notebook | eugeneyan.com, lilianweng.github.io, jalammar.github.io | Source Serif 4 / Newsreader body, mono for numbers | Near-white, ink, one link color | Numbered sections, sidenotes, margin figures, eval tables | Reads as a blog, not a hire-me page, if used for the homepage |
| 3 | Dark technical, without the clichés | Linear, Vercel-dark, delba.dev | Geist + Geist Mono | `#0a0a0a`→`#18191a` surfaces, text `#ededed`/`#8a8f98`, hairline `#23252a`, non-purple accent | Single column or restrained two-column | Highest template risk (shadcn, Brittany Chiang clones); low-contrast gray-on-black |
| 4 | Light product landing, subtle motion | Vercel-light, paco.me | Geist / Inter + mono | `#fafafa` / `#171717` / `#ebebeb` | Each project as a mini product with one live interaction (waveform, streaming tokens) | Drifts into SaaS sameness; motion costs bytes |
| — | Bento, terminal, 3D/OS | Templates, terminal.satnaing.dev, bruno-simon.com | — | — | — | Clichéd or hard to skim; the medieval UI already covers "fun" |

### Direction 1: warm editorial lab notebook (recommended default)

This is the strongest choice for three reasons. It visually links the candidate to the labs he wants to work alongside. It stands apart from the dark-navy and purple crowd. And it ages best, because it is the style most prestige builders have settled on. Use a serif display face for the name and section headings (Instrument Serif at display sizes, or Newsreader if serif also carries the reading text), Geist or IBM Plex Sans for UI, and JetBrains Mono only for metrics, stack tags and the ⌘K hint. Avoiding Inter as the only face matters because Inter by default "signals no typography decision was made" ([925 Studios](https://www.925studios.co/blog/ai-slop-design-tells)). Pick an accent tied to something true about him rather than a Tailwind default. Warm rust or amber suits a Hyderabad identity without being decorative. Follow Anthropic's rule of keeping dark surfaces for "product chrome": the code snippets, the latency-budget table and the chatbot panel sit on a near-black well, which makes the AI work look like real instrumentation. The hero should be text, not an image. It needs name, "AI Engineer," and one specific line such as "I build voice agents that answer in under 800 ms" (only if that number is true), plus resume, GitHub and email links. Then come 3–4 featured case studies, experience, writing and contact. Practitioner guidance agrees that **3–5 deep projects beat 10+ shallow ones** ([DEV checklist](https://dev.to/_d7eb1c1703182e3ce1782/developer-portfolio-checklist-20-things-hiring-managers-look-for-388p)).

### Direction 2: research notebook for the case-study pages

On its own, this Tufte-style direction looks too much like a blog to be the homepage of someone who is job-hunting. As the **template for each project page**, though, it is ideal. Use numbered sections (Problem → Architecture → Decisions and trade-offs → Evals → Results → What I'd do next), a serif reading body, sidenotes for caveats, margin figures for architecture diagrams, and monospace tables for p50/p95 latency, recall@k and cost per call. This borrows Jay Alammar's visual explanations and Eugene Yan's mix of prototypes and essays ([jalammar.github.io](https://jalammar.github.io); [eugeneyan.com](https://eugeneyan.com)). It also shows the eval-centred thinking that hiring managers say almost nobody demonstrates. Directions 1 and 2 share a type system, so together they form one coherent site, not two styles.

### Direction 3: dark technical, offered as the dark theme

The trend listicles that call dark mode "the default" for developer portfolios are low-authority ([Envato](https://elements.envato.com/learn/portfolio-trends)). The usability evidence points the other way for a default. NN/g found **light mode beats dark for readability in normal-vision users**, with the gap widening at small font sizes, and recommends light by default with a dark option ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/dark-mode/)). People with astigmatism or dyslexia can find dark text harder to read ([Gadget Hacks](https://android.gadgethacks.com/news/dark-mode-vs-light-mode-what-research-says-about-when-each-works-best/)). Recruiters mostly read in bright offices. So treat Linear-style dark as the toggle state of Direction 1, using Claude's warm dark surfaces `#181715`/`#1f1e1b`/`#252320` rather than cold slate. Keep the same single accent. Check every gray against WCAG's **4.5:1** contrast ratio ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html)). Low-contrast text was on **83.9% of the top million home pages in 2026** ([WebAIM](https://webaim.org/projects/million/)), and glassy dark AI themes are where it shows up most. As a standalone default this direction ranks third because of the template risk.

### Direction 4: light product landing with one live interaction

Use this if he wants each project to feel like a shipped product. Each case study gets a hero panel with a single honest interaction: an audio waveform from a real voice-agent call, or a token stream replaying a real RAG answer. Motion should be limited to reveal-on-scroll and that one element. It is credible, but it drifts toward the homogeneity of SaaS sites ("product UI mockups floating on soft gradients") ([Overpass Studio](https://www.overpass.studio/blog/why-saas-websites-look-the-same)), and every animation adds JavaScript. The better use of its best idea, a live artifact per project, is inside Direction 1's dark chrome panels.

## The chatbot should look like a command palette and answer like a RAG system

For an AI engineer, the chatbot is not decoration. It is a **live demo of the skill he is selling**, and that cuts both ways. Portfolio chatbots get attention: CNBC profiled two job seekers whose recruiter-facing bots "opened up a lot of opportunities," using suggested prompts like "Give me a quick summary" ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/amp/2026/04/30/these-2-job-seekers-built-ai-chatbots-to-talk-to-recruiters-for-them.html)). A Show HN resume bot drew praise and interest from a recruiter. Its creator admitted that real questions often got poor answers despite hundreds of prompts in a day ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=39265393)). "Ask-my-resume" bots are now common in junior GitHub portfolios, and none of the elite AI sites reviewed embed one ([kerilpatel](https://github.com/kerilpatel/portfolio-ai-chatbot); [dennisglouki](https://github.com/dennisglouki/Chatbot_CV)). A generic bot is a commodity. A bad answer is a visible negative signal. A fast, grounded bot that cites its sources is proof of skill.

Presentation should follow the established professional pattern for AI entry points: the **⌘K command palette** used by Linear, Figma, Notion, Vercel and Raycast ([uxpatterns.dev](https://uxpatterns.dev/patterns/advanced/command-palette); [AI UX Playground](https://aiuxplayground.com/pattern/command-bar/)). Paco Coursey and swyx both put ⌘K on their personal sites ([paco.me](https://paco.me); [swyx.io](https://swyx.io)). In the professional UI, place an input-styled line in the hero, such as "Ask my portfolio anything ⌘K," with a monospace key hint. It opens a centered panel about 640px wide on the dark chrome surface, with a hairline border and a plain dim backdrop (no glass blur). Inside, offer 3–4 suggested prompts ("What voice agents has he shipped?", "Show the RAG eval numbers," "Latency budget?"). Stream the answers and include **citations that deep-link to case-study sections**, which shows RAG competence directly. The same palette also serves as site navigation (Projects, Resume, Contact), which justifies having it. On mobile it becomes a bottom sheet. Skip avatar bubbles, sparkle icons and gradient borders; Creative Boom names these as fatigued ([Creative Boom](https://www.creativeboom.com/insight/10-trends-creatives-are-so-over-in-2026/)). The bot must stay optional, so all content remains readable without it. It needs guards against prompt injection and abuse. The repository's recent commit already rate-limits the chat API.

In the medieval UI, the same backend can sit behind a themed persona (an oracle or scribe). That makes the switch between UIs feel like a deliberate demonstration of one system with two front ends, not two separate projects. One small eval page showing his golden set and pass rate for the portfolio bot would turn the bot from a gimmick into a piece of evidence. This is the eval signal hiring managers say is rare ([hireagentic.dev](https://hireagentic.dev/blog/hire-ai-engineer-guide)).

## Speed and restraint are themselves the craft signal

He is applying globally from Hyderabad, so reviewers will open the site on unpredictable networks and devices. Google's data shows bounce probability rises **32% as load time goes from 1 s to 3 s, and 90% at 5 s** ([Think with Google](https://business.google.com/ca-en/think/marketing-strategies/mobile-page-speed-new-industry-benchmarks/)). The data is from 2017, but it still points the right way. The targets are Core Web Vitals "good": **LCP ≤ 2.5 s, INP ≤ 200 ms, CLS ≤ 0.1** ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/vitals)).

In Next.js 14 this is easy to reach if the design stays restrained. `next/font` self-hosts Google fonts with no layout shift ([Next.js docs](https://nextjs.org/docs/app/getting-started/fonts)). On Next 14, Geist should come from the `geist` npm package with `transpilePackages: ['geist']` ([npm](https://www.npmjs.com/package/geist)). A blocking inline `<head>` script that sets `data-theme` before paint prevents the dark-mode flash without giving up static rendering ([Next.js](https://nextjs.org/docs/app/guides/preventing-flash-before-hydration)). This is especially relevant here because the same mechanism can carry the professional/medieval switch. If the chat panel needs animation, `LazyMotion` with `m` brings Motion's initial cost from about 34 kB down to about 4.6 kB ([Motion docs](https://motion.dev/docs/react-reduce-bundle-size)). Load the palette with `next/dynamic` on the first ⌘K press so it never affects LCP. CSS custom-property tokens in plain CSS also keep the site clear of Tailwind's indigo default, where the purple cliché comes from. Many of these limits (two font families plus mono, a text-only hero, under about 100 kB of first-load JS) are budgets inferred from the evidence above, not published requirements.

Some uncertainty remains. No study measures which portfolio aesthetic gets more interviews, and no study tracks recruiter behaviour on engineer sites specifically. The ranking rests on credibility research, hiring-manager reports, what admired practitioners actually do, and design-community sentiment. Those sources agree, but they are not controlled evidence. The "warm editorial" pull of AI labs is also partly fashion, and fashion turns over. The case for Direction 1 rests mostly on its restraint and readability, so it holds up even if cream backgrounds go out of style.

## Conclusion

The medieval UI changes the problem. Because the site already has a memorable, playful mode, the professional default is free to be the most boring-looking thing on the site and the most evidence-dense, and that combination is what senior AI hiring managers reward. The ranked recommendation is: **(1) warm editorial lab notebook as the default**, with a single non-purple accent and dark "chrome" panels; **(2) research-notebook case-study pages** carrying architecture diagrams, eval tables and latency budgets; **(3) a warm-dark variant of the same system** as the theme toggle rather than a separate style; **(4) product-landing touches** only as one real live artifact per project. Bento, terminal and 3D styles should be left to the medieval side.

The biggest lever is not visual at all. It is the chatbot, positioned as a ⌘K palette that cites its sources, with a published eval of its own accuracy. Most portfolios either lack a bot or ship an ungrounded one. A measured bot turns the site from a page that describes AI engineering into a working example of it, and the dual-UI switch shows that one backend can drive two front ends. Presented that way, the switch reads as system design rather than a gimmick.
